# [SPEC] Validation loop — the stop trigger, the finding bar, and the validation record

## 1. Intent and Executive Summary

Single-sourcing discipline for this artifact: each operative fact is stated once in the section that owns it — the problem statement owns the defect and its shape, the scope table owns what changes on which surface by pointing at the requirement that defines the change, the requirements own the rules, the success criteria own the checks against those rules, and the evidence owns measured history. Sections point at owners instead of restating them; the artifact's own loop is the measured failure of the opposite shape.

| 1 | Defect and shape | The deck's validation loops — the spec card's and plan card's validation cycles and the verify card's review loop — stop on one trigger only: recurrence, the spin judgment, *"findings recurring or reshuffling without genuine resolution."* Three further defects leave the loop's other failure shapes unbound: the finding/preference boundary's determinant is unstated, so each round adjudicated "affecting correctness" by effect size (`.opencode#2582`: the same stale-line-number-citation class was finding F2 in round 1 and a disclosed non-finding inside a clean PASS in round 5, against a bar whose disclosure path had been abolished three days earlier by `.opencode` commit `b7ace773`, 2026-10-10); no record makes rounds comparable after the fact (`.opencode#2658` and `#2657` hold no audit verdict records — the rounds survive only as commit subjects); and `#2606`'s retirement of the one-review bound left one surface unamended (`implementation-workflow.md`'s verify-pass row still carries *"one bounded review"*). (c) Capping rounds outright and escalating at the count — discarded: escalation without re-evaluation shifts a decomposition fault the agent is responsible for onto the developer. (d) Scripting the re-evaluation — discarded: the examination's verdict is intent-decidable; the factors name the minimum and no more. (3) The factors are the named minimum, exemplary and open; their outcomes are recorded, so a trigger that fires and produces nothing is visible. The reconciliation carried: the count enters as a mandatory trigger that decides nothing; the judgment keeps the outcome. All skill cards are in scope for revisions. Blast radius: the surfaces in §2.1; no tool, harness, hook, or script surface changes.

## 3. Requirements

- **R-1 — The stop trigger.** Each of the three loop locations SHALL carry, alongside the spin judgment, a **non-convergence judgment**: when the loop's rounds are incrementally progressing — each cycle remediating its findings yet producing genuinely new ones, with no obvious convergence — the agent running the loop SHALL stop in-place revision and run a structural re-evaluation of the artifact before any further in-place revision. The trigger is a judgment stated as such, never scripted. Alongside it, a **count trigger**: when the artifact has needed more than four revisions to address findings, the loop SHALL run the structural re-evaluation before any further in-place revision. The count mandates the examination and never decides its outcome — the re-evaluation's verdict is the judgment, and a re-evaluation may conclude that the artifact stands.
- **R-2 — The factor set.** The structural re-evaluation SHALL examine, at minimum: **repo boundaries**; **separation of concerns**; **atomic work-unit decomposition**; **fatness**; and **intent wording/reachability**. The factors are the minimum the examination covers — exemplary and open, never a closed checklist — and the examination and its verdict are judgment. The plan card's validation standards and the verify card's review loop SHALL each carry or point to that canonical factor set, so the minimum is reachable from every loop the rule governs.
- **R-3 — The outcomes.** The re-evaluation SHALL resolve to one of three outcomes, each recorded, from which the loop resumes or redirects: (a) **Sound** — the artifact's structure stands: the findings are recorded and the loop resumes on the unchanged artifact; (b) **Recompose** — the artifact's shape is the defect: the split is performed per the recomposition protocol the issues card carries, and in-place revision of the pre-split artifact is superseded; (c) **Intent-unreachable** — the intent statement is the defect: it is rewritten, and the loop continues on the revised artifact. A re-evaluation is one of the higher-level fixing attempts the existing escalation rule counts; escalation still fires when convergence remains unreachable after it. The recomposition protocol is specified in the sibling `.opencode#2661`: while it is unimplemented, the Recompose outcome records its grounds and escalates to the developer for the split — the escalation the existing rule already carries.
- **R-4 — Where the outcome goes.** The re-evaluation's outcome and its grounds SHALL be recorded in the owning issue's validation record — never in the artifact's body.
- **R-5 — The validation record.** The verify card's §3 recording rule and the per-issue validation record are reconciled by this change: the deliverable's verdict is recorded once, in chat or the PR description, and the validation record is the loop's history — never a second copy of the verdict, never an artifact chain. Every completed validation cycle — one fresh-context review of the artifact as it then stood, its verdict, its findings, and the remediation or loop state they produced — SHALL append one entry to the owning issue's validation record (YAML) at `.issues/{N}/audit/validation.yaml`: one record file per issue, the owning issue being the issue whose artifact the loop validates; one top-level section per artifact under validation (spec, plan, implementation); each entry carrying the round's findings, every defect class the round adjudicated **with its classification** (a finding or a preference) and, for a finding, its remediation performed or the outcome reached, including the terminal cycle; and, when a cycle ran the structural re-evaluation, the re-evaluation's outcome and its grounds. The recorded class is the criteria source's enumerated defect class or analysis family wherever one applies, and the recorded factual kinds (a stale citation, a wrong count, a mis-scoped claim) are the entry's detail, exemplars of the class rather than a closed list; an adjudicated preference records the class or family it was adjudicated under with its classification; the criteria source's own class or family is the label wherever one applies, and the same defect carries the same label in every round, so that two rounds' entries for one defect compare. A round whose entry lists no finding is a zero-finding round. The record lives beside the artifact in the issue store and never inside the spec or plan body. A cycle whose artifact belongs to no issue in a store — the work card's short-path scope statement — records its verdict per the verify card's own recording rule and appends no store record. A cycle's judgment cites the record.
- **R-6 — The finding bar.** Both criteria sources' verdict contracts, and the verify card's §3 verdict contract, SHALL state the finding/preference boundary as class-determined: a **defect the criteria source enumerates** — any of its enumerated defect classes or analysis families, whether or not it misstates fact — is a finding, and within that bucket a **factual defect** (a statement that misstates fact) is a finding regardless of how small its effect is; a **preference** — style, phrasing taste, a hypothetical improvement — is never a finding; and no third category exists: a substantive observation is reported as a finding, and nothing is recorded as an observation without being a finding. The same defect class SHALL be classified the same way in every round, and the validation record's per-round classification of each adjudicated class is what makes that checkable. The churn rule's verbatim form is unchanged. Where the audited artifact is a spec or a plan, its "correctness" prong means the artifact's factual accuracy and the completeness of its impact enumeration — a defect in either is a factual defect, classified by its class like any other. Where the audited artifact is a code deliverable, the churn rule's prong keeps its existing meaning — the deliverable's behavior, and the defects that affect correctness or the stated requirements — the redefinition does not narrow it.
- **R-7 — The bars stand.** The findings-are-fixed requirement — each revision genuinely fixes every named finding; findings are never negotiated away — and criteria invariance SHALL stand unchanged, and SHALL never be satisfied by a round count.
- **R-8 — The stale gloss.** The implementation-workflow reference's verify-pass row SHALL describe its step consistently with the verify card's review loop — the step reaches a zero-finding verdict under the verify card's FAIL path — and SHALL no longer state a bound of one review.

## 6. Evidence

History only — the rules and checks live with their R-numbers and SC-numbers.

**The measured instance.** `.opencode#2658` ran **ten** validation rounds — commit subjects on `2658/spec.md` carry "validation round 1" through "validation round 10" (rounds 2 and 3 each carry two commits with identical subjects; fifteen non-automated commits in all), and the issue's store folder holds no audit verdict records (checked 2026-10-23). Pre-recomposition the spec was 221 lines with twelve requirements and twelve criteria, and its evidence section spanned 114 of the 221 lines — 81 of the file's 163 non-blank lines. The late rounds' remediation subjects name description-level corrections: title-reference counts, exhibit-marker cells, a duplicated impact paragraph, interval notation, a ticket-reference claim, enumeration consistency in the approach and scope tables. The recomposition came from the developer's intervention (store commit `c81089a5`, 2026-10-23: *"evidence moved to a research artifact, store-record repairs split out to #2659, normative spec reduced to ten requirements on three cards"*), reduced the spec to 122 lines with ten requirements and ten criteria, and resolved the remaining findings at once. The loop's detector never fired.

**The classification divergence.** `.opencode#2582`'s five audit records show one loop whose `claims_sampled` grew 17 → 13 → 44 → 36 → 65 across rounds. Round 1's F2 — *"other correction/factual error (stale line-number citation)"* — was a finding. Round 5's notes disclose two *"sub-churn line-range drifts… off by a few lines with correct content, no effect on correctness or stated requirements"* as non-findings and exit with a clean PASS. Same defect class, opposite classification, three days after the disclosure path was abolished (`b7ace773`, 2026-10-10: *"verdict contract — zero-findings PASS bar, automatic FAIL on any reported finding, non-finding-note path abolished"*).

**Why judgment, not a count alone.** `.opencode#2606` bounded the loop after discarding counters and numeric bounds (its Alternative (c): a counter *"cannot judge whether findings persist because remediation was fake or because the artifact carries a deeper defect"*), and the floor rules that fact-decidable rules may be enforced mechanically while intent-decidable ones are never scripted. `.opencode#2657`'s loop left 24 remediation commits across two subject-label schemes whose subjects name substantive distinct repairs — *"R-3 rebuilt on decidability"*, *"criteria stated once"*, *"deck open-choice rule unified"* — and whose content the deck's work card now carries in its provenance. What separates the shape-defective loop from the shape-sound one is the remediation's character, not its count; only judgment reads that character, and the record is what lets it read honestly.

**This artifact's own loop, as the count's measured case.** Under the count trigger as amended this session, this spec ran **nine validation dispatches, 28 findings, ten revisions to address defects** — every finding remediated in place, the loop's shape unchanged; the deck's cards were rewritten by a concurrent session between rounds 8 and 9 of the sibling `.opencode#2662`'s loop, and two of that artifact's round-8 claims were mis-sourced against the rewritten cards. The record: `.opencode/.issues/2660/audit/re-evaluation.yaml` (two re-evaluation runs — the first concluded sound and was falsified by the five rounds after it; the second concludes recompose, grounds recorded, escalated to the developer per R-3's precondition). The recurrence-keyed trigger did not fire on the loop-of-genuine-repairs shape at any point.
