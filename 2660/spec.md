# [SPEC] Validation loop — non-convergence judgment, the factual-defect bar, and the validation record

Resolves `.opencode#2660`.

## 1. Intent and Executive Summary

| # | Field | Description |
|---|-------|-------------|
| 1 | **Problem Statement** | The deck's validation loops — the spec card's and plan card's validation cycles and the verify card's review loop — carry a spin judgment keyed on **repetition**: *"the agent running the loop judges — never a script — whether the loop is spinning: findings recurring or reshuffling without genuine resolution relative to the remediation performed"* (`validation-standards.md` §Loop, `plan-validation-standards.md` §Loop, `verify/SKILL.md` §4). A loop whose findings are genuinely new each round never reads as spinning, however many rounds run. The measured instance is `.opencode#2658`: ten validation rounds whose remediations name citation counts, exhibit-marker table cells, duplicated paragraphs, interval notation, and claim rescope — ending in a recomposition the developer's intervention reached and the loop's own judgment never did. Three further defects compound it. First, the finding/preference boundary is adjudicated round by round by effect size: in `.opencode#2582`, the same defect class — a stale line-range citation — was finding F2 in round 1 ("stale line-number citation") and a disclosed **non-finding** in round 5 ("sub-churn line-range drifts… off by a few lines with correct content, no effect on correctness or stated requirements"), inside a clean PASS — against a bar that had abolished the disclosure path in `.opencode` commit `b7ace773` (2026-10-10). Second, nothing makes rounds comparable after the fact: `.opencode#2658` and `.opencode#2657` have no audit verdict records — their rounds survive only as git commit subjects — so the loop's per-cycle judgment has no evidence base beyond the running agent's session memory. Third, the retirement #2606 performed was incomplete on one of the loop family's own surfaces: the `implement` card's implementation-workflow verify-pass row still describes the review step as bounded to one review — the pre-#2606 bound that #2606 retired from the loop's other surfaces. |
| 2 | **Root Cause / Motivation** | Three rule defects and one evidence defect. (1) The loop's stop trigger keys on repetition only: `.opencode#2606` bounded the loop with the semantic spin condition after discarding counters and numeric bounds (its Alternative (c)), leaving the loop's other failure shape — steady genuinely-new shallow findings with no convergence — without a trigger. (2) The reviewer bar's boundary determinant is unstated: the churn binding says the reviewer flags only gaps *"affecting correctness or the stated requirements"*, and an effect-size reading of "affecting correctness" lets a small factual defect be classified as a disclosed non-finding — #2575 item 1 abolished the disclosure path (`b7ace773`), but the boundary's determinant — the defect's class, not its effect — was never stated, and `plan-validation-standards.md` carries no equivalent of the observation-is-a-finding line at all. Minor factual defects that are noticed and consciously not fixed compound — the developer's ruling: *"minor defects compound into serious flaws"*. (3) The loop's comparative judgments have no record to judge from: rounds live in git commit subjects and session memory. (4) #2606's retirement of the one-review bound did not reach every surface that stated it: the `implement` card's implementation-workflow reference still carries *"one bounded review"* in its verify-pass row — wording of the pre-#2606 bound, contradicting the loop the verify card now states. (5) The trigger's reach: a judgment asking whether the loop converges cannot fire on a loop whose every round is an individually genuine repair, because each round reads as progress — the shape the developer's 2026-10-23/24 restatement names outright (*"if a spec or plan needs more than 4 revisions to address defects, it needs to be re-evaluated"*), and the shape this issue's own successor instance exhibited (`.opencode#2662`: thirteen fresh-context validation dispatches, twelve returning findings and the last a clean PASS, 40 findings in total — every finding remediated in place, the loop's shape unchanged). The instance's record is `.opencode/.issues/2662/audit/re-evaluation.yaml` with the store's comment record alongside it. A count of revisions is a fact-decidable observation of the loop's own history, and it fires on that shape where the judgment cannot; the developer's restatement is the basis for carrying it alongside the judgment. |
| 3 | **Approach Chosen** | Four changes, in the three loop homes (`validation-standards.md` §Loop canonical, `plan-validation-standards.md` §Loop, `verify/SKILL.md` §4) plus one stale gloss: (1) a **non-convergence judgment** beside the spin judgment — when rounds are incrementally progressing, each remediating its findings yet producing genuinely new ones with no obvious convergence, the revision step stops in-place revision and runs a **structural re-evaluation** of the artifact — and, alongside it, a **count trigger**: more than four revisions to address findings mandates the same re-evaluation before any further in-place revision, the count deciding nothing and the re-evaluation free to conclude the artifact stands; (2) named minimum re-evaluation factors — repo boundaries, separation of concerns, atomic work-unit decomposition, fatness and the recomposition it may call for, intent wording/reachability — with three recorded outcomes and the loop resuming or redirecting from each; (3) the **factual-defect bar** — the finding/preference boundary is the defect's class, stated in both criteria sources' verdict contracts, no third category; (4) a **validation record** — every completed validation cycle appends one entry to the owning issue's record at `.issues/{N}/audit/validation.yaml`, the judgment's evidence base, kept outside the artifact bodies. The `implement` card's implementation-workflow verify-pass row stops describing the review step as bounded to one review — a pre-#2606 residue. Judgment stays judgment throughout: no script. The count is a mandate to examine, never a verdict — the discipline that discarded counters as verdicts in #2606, with the developer's 2026-10-23/24 restatement carried alongside the judgment. |
| 4 | **Alternatives Considered & Why Discarded** | (a) **Keep the revision-count trigger this issue was first filed with as the loop's sole trigger** — discarded: #2606 discarded counters and numeric bounds for loop control, the floor rules that fact-decidable rules may be enforced mechanically while intent-decidable ones are never, and a count alone maps badly onto the measured cases — `.opencode#2657`'s ~23 recorded revision commits name substantive, distinct semantic repairs (its content now lives in the deck's work card), so what distinguishes the shape-defective loop from the shape-sound one is the remediation's character, which only judgment reads. **Carried, amended, on the developer's 2026-10-23/24 restatement: the count returns as a mandatory trigger that opens the structural re-evaluation** — the observation that fires on a loop whose every round is an individually genuine repair, where the judgment cannot — while the judgment keeps the examination's verdict, and the re-evaluation remains free to conclude that the artifact stands. (b) **Extend the spin judgment only, with no re-evaluation trigger** — discarded: the measured failure is the loop's opposite — genuinely new findings never read as spinning — so renaming nothing leaves the stop condition unbounded for the measured shape. (c) **Cap rounds outright and escalate the developer at a count** — discarded: escalation without recomposition shifts a decomposition fault the agent is responsible for fixing onto the developer; the trigger must open a re-evaluation, not a halt. (d) **Script the re-evaluation** — factor checklists, size metrics, tooling — discarded: the examination's verdict is intent-decidable; the factors name the minimum it covers and no more than that is stated. (e) **State the rule in the spec card's criteria source only** — discarded: the plan's loop and the verify card's loop are the same loop family since #2606; a rule one location does not carry does not bind. (f) **Keep a third bucket — observations disclosed without failing the round** — discarded: the disclosure path was abolished by #2575 item 1, and #2582's round-5 PASS shows the path survives wherever the classification's determinant goes unstated; the developer's compounding ruling forbids the noted-but-unfixed bucket. (g) **Record rounds inside the spec or plan body** — discarded: the body footer allowlist (spec card §5) forbids process and tracking content; the record lives beside the artifact. (h) **One record file per round** (the `#2582` `audit/` shape) — discarded: per-round files make one artifact's history a naming convention rather than a record; the per-issue record with per-round entries is append-only and comparable without naming rules. |
| 5 | **Key Design Decisions** | (1) One judgment step, two failure modes: non-convergence is judged beside spin, per cycle, by the agent running the loop — never a script (the #2606 discipline). (2) The trigger opens a re-evaluation; it does not end the loop — the zero-finding verdict and the existing escalation remain the loop's exits, and a re-evaluation counts as one of the higher-level fixing attempts the escalation rule counts. (3) The re-evaluation's factors are the named minimum (the developer's list), exemplary and open — the cause-class discipline #2606 recorded; its outcomes are named and recorded, so a trigger that fires and produces nothing is visible in the record. (4) The Sound outcome preserves the developer's boundary — the re-evaluation *"does not have to make changes to the spec to result in valid findings and a resumption of the audit/revise loop"*. (5) The bar has two buckets and a stated determinant: a factual defect is reported however small its effect; a preference is never reported; classification follows the defect's class — stable across rounds — and the record makes the stability checkable. (6) The record is per issue, append-only, section-scoped per artifact (spec, plan, implementation), outside the artifact body, marked `(YAML)` per the verify card's agent-to-agent format rule, and is what makes the per-cycle judgment evidence-based rather than memory-based. (7) One mechanism, statements per surface (the #2597/#2606 pattern): `validation-standards.md` §Loop states the mechanics canonically; the plan reference states the loop's own instance; the verify card states the review loop's instance. (8) Root-agnostic: the rule names no repository or artifact-specific size; the record path is the deck's `.issues/{N}/audit/validation.yaml` convention. |
| 6 | **User Intent / Original Prompt** | Session 2026-10-23. The developer observed that *the agent may be getting stuck in long performative revise/audit loops on specs and plans*, first stating the trigger as a revision count — *"if a spec or plan needs more than 4 revisions to address defects, it needs to be re-evaluated for proper decomposition and recomposition… taking into account things like repo boundaries, separation of concerns, atomic work unit decomposition and recomposition needing re-evaluation, or the spec being too fat and needing to split up into multiple inter-dependent specs among other possible issues… it may also be an indicator that the spec's intent is poorly worded or not reachable"* — then correcting the form: the trigger *"doesn't need to be a fixed count, but incrementally progressing with no obvious convergence is an issue"*, and a re-evaluation *"does not have to make changes to the spec to result in valid findings and a resumption of the audit/revise loop"*; on the reviewer bar: *"minor defects compound into serious flaws"* — no noted-but-unfixed bucket; and *"all skill cards are in scope for potential revisions"*. The recomposition workflow is split out as the sibling `.opencode#2661`; the directive to perform the split rather than propose it, and its authorization ruling, are recorded there. The revision-count trigger was restated by the developer three times in the session of 2026-10-23/24 while `.opencode#2662` sat in its own validation loop; the restatement is the basis for carrying the count as a mandatory trigger that opens the re-evaluation, and the deck's earlier correction — the trigger *"doesn't need to be a fixed count"* and a re-evaluation *"does not have to make changes to the spec to result in valid findings and a resumption of the audit/revise loop"* — is preserved in that the count decides nothing and the re-evaluation is free to find the artifact sound. |

## 3. Requirements

- **R-1** — Each of the three loop locations SHALL carry, alongside the spin judgment, a **non-convergence judgment**: when the loop's rounds are incrementally progressing — each cycle remediating its findings yet producing genuinely new ones, with no obvious convergence — the agent running the loop SHALL stop in-place revision and run a structural re-evaluation of the artifact before any further in-place revision. The trigger is a judgment stated as such, never scripted. **Alongside it, a count trigger: when the artifact has needed more than four revisions to address findings, the loop SHALL run the structural re-evaluation before any further in-place revision. The count mandates the examination and never decides its outcome — the re-evaluation's verdict is the judgment, and a re-evaluation may conclude that the artifact stands (Sound).**
- **R-2** — The structural re-evaluation SHALL examine, at minimum: **repo boundaries** (whether the artifact mixes material bounded for different repositories); **separation of concerns** (whether it mixes normative content with evidence, or record hygiene with normative change); **atomic work-unit decomposition** (whether its units are independently verifiable and sized); **fatness** (whether it should be recomposed into multiple interdependent artifacts); and **intent wording/reachability** (whether the stated intent is poorly worded or not reachable as written). The factors are the minimum the examination covers — exemplary and open, never a closed checklist — and the examination and its verdict are judgment.
- **R-3** — The re-evaluation SHALL resolve to one of three outcomes, each recorded, from which the loop resumes or redirects: (a) **Sound** — the artifact's structure stands: the re-evaluation's findings are recorded and the revise → re-validate loop resumes on the unchanged artifact; (b) **Recompose** — the artifact's shape is the defect: the split is performed per the recomposition protocol the issues card carries, and in-place revision of the pre-split artifact is superseded by the recomposition; (c) **Intent-unreachable** — the intent statement is the defect: it is rewritten, and the loop continues on the revised artifact. A re-evaluation is one of the higher-level fixing attempts the existing escalation rule counts; escalation still fires when convergence remains unreachable after it. The recomposition protocol the Recompose outcome performs the split by is specified in the sibling `.opencode#2661` and is not yet carried by the deck: while it is unimplemented, the Recompose outcome records its grounds and the loop escalates to the developer for the split, which the existing escalation rule already carries.
- **R-4** — The re-evaluation's outcome and its grounds SHALL be recorded in the owning issue's validation record (R-5) — never in the artifact's body.
- **R-5** — The `verify` card's §3 recording rule and the per-issue validation record are reconciled by the change: the deliverable's verdict is recorded once, in chat or the PR description, and the validation record is the loop's history — one appended file per issue, never a second copy of the verdict and never an artifact chain. Every completed validation cycle — one fresh-context review of the artifact as it then stood, its verdict, its findings, and the remediation or loop state they produced — SHALL append one entry to the owning issue's validation record (YAML) at `.issues/{N}/audit/validation.yaml`: one record file per issue, the owning issue being the issue whose artifact the loop validates; one top-level section per artifact under validation (spec, plan, implementation); each entry carrying the round's outcome, every defect class the round adjudicated **with its classification** (a finding or a preference) and, for a finding, its remediation performed or the outcome reached, including the terminal cycle. The classification a round records is not an observation — it is the classification the round made — and recording it is what makes the stability requirement checkable. The record lives beside the artifact in the issue store and never inside the spec or plan body — the body footer allowlist forbids process and tracking content. A cycle whose artifact belongs to no issue in a store — the `work` card's short-path scope statement — records its verdict per the verify card's own recording rule and appends no store record. A cycle's judgment cites the record.
- **R-6** — Both criteria sources' verdict contracts SHALL state the finding/preference boundary as class-determined: a **factual defect** — any statement in the artifact that misstates fact (a stale citation, a wrong count, a mis-scoped claim) — is a finding regardless of how small its effect is; a **preference** — style, phrasing taste, a hypothetical improvement — is never a finding; and no third category exists: a substantive observation is reported as a finding, and nothing is recorded as an observation without being a finding. The same defect class SHALL be classified the same way in every round, and the validation record's per-round classification of each adjudicated class is what makes that checkable. The churn rule's verbatim form is unchanged; its "correctness" content is the artifact's factual accuracy, not its blast radius.
- **R-7** — The findings-are-fixed requirement (each revision genuinely fixes every named finding; findings are never negotiated away) and criteria invariance SHALL stand unchanged, and SHALL never be satisfied by a round count.
- **R-8** — The `implement` card's implementation-workflow reference SHALL describe its verify-pass step consistently with the verify card's review loop — the step reaches a zero-finding verdict under the verify card's FAIL path — and its purpose gloss SHALL no longer state a bound of one review.

## 6. Evidence

**The measured instance.** `.opencode#2658`, the deck's closing-keyword contract, ran **ten** validation rounds — commit subjects on `2658/spec.md` carry the labels "validation round 1" through "validation round 10" (rounds 2 and 3 each carry two commits with identical subjects; fifteen non-automated commits in all), and the issue's store folder holds no audit verdict records (checked 2026-10-23). Pre-recomposition the spec was 221 lines with twelve requirements and twelve criteria, and its evidence section spanned 114 of the 221 lines — 81 of the file's 163 non-blank lines. The late rounds' remediation subjects name description-level corrections: title-reference counts, exhibit-marker cells, a duplicated impact paragraph, interval notation, a ticket-reference claim, enumeration consistency in the approach and scope tables. The recomposition came from the developer's intervention (store commit `c81089a5`, 2026-10-23: *"evidence moved to a research artifact, store-record repairs split out to #2659, normative spec reduced to ten requirements on three cards"*), reduced the spec to 122 lines with ten requirements and ten criteria, and resolved the remaining findings at once. The loop's detector never fired: the findings were genuinely new each round, so the repetition-keyed judgment read "not spinning".

**The classification divergence.** `.opencode#2582`'s five audit records show one loop whose claims_sampled grew 17 → 13 → 44 → 36 → 65 across rounds. Round 1's F2 — *"other correction/factual error (stale line-number citation)"* — was a finding. Round 5's notes disclose two *"sub-churn line-range drifts… off by a few lines with correct content, no effect on correctness or stated requirements"* as non-findings and exit with a clean PASS. Same defect class, opposite classification, and the verdict contract had abolished the disclosure path three days earlier (`b7ace773`, 2026-10-10: *"verdict contract — zero-findings PASS bar, automatic FAIL on any reported finding, non-finding-note path abolished"*). What wobbled was the determinant — unstated, so each round adjudicated "affecting correctness" by effect size.

**Why judgment, not a count.** `.opencode#2606` bounded the loop after discarding counters and numeric bounds (its Alternative (c): a counter *"cannot judge whether findings persist because remediation was fake or because the artifact carries a deeper defect"*), and the floor rules that fact-decidable rules may be enforced mechanically while intent-decidable ones are never scripted. The measured cases show why a count maps badly: `.opencode#2657`'s loop left ~23 remediation commits across two subject-label schemes (remediation rounds 3–12; revision rounds 2–10), whose subjects name substantive distinct repairs — *"R-3 rebuilt on decidability"*, *"criteria stated once"*, *"deck open-choice rule unified"* — and whose content the deck's work card now carries in its provenance. What separates the shape-defective loop from the shape-sound one is the remediation's character, not its count; only judgment reads that character, and the record is what lets it read honestly.

**The successor instance, and the count's reach.** `.opencode#2662` — the stakeholder-channel spec filed in the same session — ran thirteen fresh-context validation dispatches: twelve returned findings, the last a clean PASS, 40 findings in total, every one remediated in place, and the loop's shape unchanged; the deck's cards were themselves rewritten by a concurrent session between rounds, so later rounds found stale attributions rather than new substance. The record is `.opencode/.issues/2662/audit/re-evaluation.yaml`, with the store's comment record alongside it. The judgment on non-convergence did not fire — the rounds read as progress — and the developer's intervention restating the count trigger did. The count is the observation that sees this shape: a loop of individually genuine repairs is invisible to a judgment that asks whether the loop is converging, and visible to a count of the revisions spent.

**The record's absence today.** `.opencode#2658` and `.opencode#2657` store folders carry no audit verdict records (checked 2026-10-23) — the rounds survive only as git commit subjects and as the running agent's memory. A per-issue record gives the per-cycle judgment a ground to judge from and gives any later judgment — including this change's own audits — the stability check R-6 requires.
