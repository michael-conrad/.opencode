# [SPEC] Recomposition — performing the split, revoking superseded authorization, and notifying the developer

Resolves `.opencode#2661`.

## 1. Intent and Executive Summary

| # | Field | Description |
|---|-------|-------------|
| 1 | **Problem Statement** | When a validation loop's structural re-evaluation resolves an artifact as over-fat or mis-decomposed — the sibling `.opencode#2660`'s Recompose outcome — the deck has no protocol for performing the split. The `issues` card carries the deck's only hierarchy sentence as of 2026-10-23 — *"Link sub-issues to parents; the hierarchy carries the authorization cascade and closure order"* (`skills/issues/SKILL.md` §4) — and no card states what the cascade or the closure order concretely is, how a split is performed, or what becomes of the recomposed issue's plan, links, and authorization. Every measured split in the deck's store was ad hoc: `.opencode#2210/#2211`, `.opencode#2622/#2623`, and `.opencode#2658/#2659` all carry empty link records (checked 2026-10-23), and `.opencode#2658`'s recomposition (2026-10-23) redistributed content with no registered parent/sub relationship. That instance could not answer the questions a post-approval split makes live — does implementation authorization survive the split, what happens to an existing plan, in which order do which issues close, who is told — because no rule states any of them. |
| 2 | **Root Cause / Motivation** | The deck's splits were decided at authoring time, one instance at a time, and their mechanics were never lifted into a rule. The consequences compound in a post-approval split, where three state questions become live with no answers: (1) authorization — an implementer holding a valid authorization for the unsplit item has none for the parts, and no card says so; (2) the plan — a plan derives from its spec (plan card §4), and a recomposed spec leaves the plan deriving from an artifact that no longer exists as such, with no supersession rule anywhere in the plan card; (3) closure and links — the hierarchy sentence names a closure order without stating it, and none of the store's split pairs registered its relationship. The split can also be an escape hatch in the wrong hands: performed silently mid-implementation, it reshapes authorized work under an authorization that was granted for a different shape. |
| 3 | **Approach Chosen** | A recomposition protocol, planted in three cards. In the `issues` card beside the hierarchy sentence: the split mechanics — one issue per part, registered as a sub-issue of the recomposed issue via the `local-issues` tool's `link`; the recomposed issue's spec becomes the **umbrella** recording the parts, the seams between them, and the re-evaluation's grounds, with the redistributed content deleted from it in the same action; each part's spec reads standalone and records its side of the seams. In the `work` card's authorization check: implementation or PR authorization carried for the pre-split scope is **void** at recomposition — the developer is notified and states the parts' authorization. In the `plan` card: a plan derived from the pre-split spec is **superseded** — new plans derive from the parts' specs, delivered items keep their delivered state. The notification is a stakeholder comment on the recomposed issue carrying the recomposed issue's and each part's issue and artifacts URLs and the authorization consequence. The review shape defers to the PR contract; closure runs parts-first, then the recomposed issue closes as a verdict-backed closure recording the redistribution. No part count and no size threshold: the protocol governs mechanics only — which parts an over-fat artifact decomposes into is the loop rule's judgment. |
| 4 | **Alternatives Considered & Why Discarded** | (a) **Leave the split ad hoc, as the measured instances did** — discarded: the state questions (authorization, plan, closure, notification) have no answers in the deck, and the instance that produced them was a spec-phase recomposition with nothing to void; the first post-approval split faces them with no rule. (b) **Make the parts siblings of the recomposed issue instead of sub-issues** — discarded: the hierarchy sentence exists precisely to carry the cascade and closure order, and siblings leave the recomposed issue's own scope without a home; sub-issues state both the containment and the order. (c) **Have parts inherit the developer's existing authorization** — discarded: an authorization binds the scope it named; inheritance lets a split reshape authorized work silently — the developer's authorization decision moves after the split (R-3, R-5). (d) **Keep the pre-split plan and re-map its items to the parts** — discarded: a plan derives from its spec; re-mapping fabricated traces to artifacts that no longer exist, and partially-satisfied plans reintroduce exactly the mapping ambiguity the re-evaluation judged unworkable. (e) **Record the recomposition only in the local store** — discarded: the scope change and its authorization consequence are decisions a stakeholder must see; the issues card's comment gate exists for exactly this class, and chat is not durable. (f) **Let each part open its own PR** — discarded: the PR contract already governs — issues authorized together share one branch and one PR carrying one squashed commit per issue; a second PR rule re-creates the conflict one level down. (g) **State a part-count or size bound alongside the protocol** — discarded: the decomposition judgment belongs to the loop rule; a size metric here would re-introduce the count discipline the sibling spec discarded. |
| 5 | **Key Design Decisions** | (1) **Umbrella, not deletion**: the recomposed issue keeps its issue identity and becomes the parts' parent, with its spec reduced to parts, seams, and grounds — the redistribution is recorded, not erased. (2) **Same-action deletion**: the redistributed content leaves the umbrella spec in the same action that records the recomposition — self-containment, no superseded-content pointers (spec card §5). (3) **Authorization voids; delivery stands**: work delivered under a then-valid authorization stays delivered; nothing delivered is retroactively un-authorized, and everything further waits for the developer's stated authorization. (4) **Plan supersession, not migration**: delivered items keep their state in the implementation history; plan state never migrates to the parts. (5) **Durable notification**: a stakeholder comment on the recomposed issue — the recomposed and parts URLs plus the authorization consequence — placed per the issues card's comment gate. (6) **PR shape by deferral**: the protocol adds no second rule; `git-workflow-pr` §3's one-branch-one-PR-per-scope contract governs, with one squashed commit per part inside it. (7) **Closure order through the hierarchy**: parts close first, each by its own delivered work or triage verdict; the recomposed issue closes last, as a verdict-backed closure whose record states the redistribution — giving the hierarchy sentence the content it names. (8) **No numeric thresholds**: the protocol is mechanics-only. (9) **Root-agnostic**: the protocol names no repository and no artifact type beyond the issue store itself. |
| 6 | **User Intent / Original Prompt** | The split path was named in the developer's directive for the sibling spec — *"repo boundaries, separation of concerns, atomic work unit decomposition and recomposition needing re-evaluation, or the spec being too fat and needing to split up into multiple inter-dependent specs"* — and this session's directive made it a performed workflow, not a proposal: file this spec as `.opencode#2661`, *"cross-reference #2660 as peer sibling (one PR)"*, record the protocol's authorization ruling as the revocation of any implementation or PR authorization carried for a split scope — stating explicitly, as this session's case was, when none exists to revoke — and report to the developer with both issues' and artifacts' URLs. |

## 3. Requirements

- **R-1** — When the loop rule's structural re-evaluation resolves an artifact to recomposition, the agent running that loop SHALL perform the split: create one issue per part artifact and register each part as a sub-issue of the recomposed issue via the `local-issues` tool's `link`. Each part's spec reads standalone under the spec card's own discipline and records its side of the seams the umbrella names.
- **R-2** — The recomposed issue's spec SHALL become the umbrella: it records the parts, the seams between them (the shared conventions, interfaces, and ordering constraints a part builds on), and the re-evaluation's grounds; the redistributed content is deleted from it in the same action that records the recomposition — no superseded-content pointers remain.
- **R-3** — Implementation or PR authorization carried for the pre-split scope SHALL be void at recomposition and SHALL NOT authorize the parts. Work already delivered under a then-valid authorization stays delivered. The parts' further implementation and PR work proceeds only under the authorization the developer states after the notification (R-5). A recomposition reached during the spec phase — before any implementation or PR authorization exists — has nothing to void, and the notification still applies.
- **R-4** — A plan derived from the pre-split spec SHALL be superseded at recomposition. New plans derive from the parts' specs. Delivered items keep their delivered state in the work's implementation history, and plan state SHALL NOT migrate to the parts.
- **R-5** — The agent performing the recomposition SHALL notify the developer at split time, carrying the recomposed issue's and each part's issue and artifacts URLs and the authorization consequence, posted as a stakeholder comment on the recomposed issue per the `issues` card's comment gate. Chat reporting accompanies the comment and does not replace it.
- **R-6** — The parts' review shape SHALL follow the PR contract: issues the developer authorizes together share one branch and one PR carrying one squashed commit per part; a part SHALL NOT open its own branch or PR outside the authorization scope that names it. The protocol states no second PR rule.
- **R-7** — Each part SHALL close by its own delivered work or its own triage verdict per the `issues` card's closure rule. The recomposed issue SHALL close only after every part has closed, as a verdict-backed closure whose record states the redistribution — the pre-split scope was superseded by the parts. The `issues` card's hierarchy sentence carries this order.
- **R-8** — The protocol SHALL state no part count and no size threshold. Which parts an over-fat artifact decomposes into, and when, is the loop rule's structural re-evaluation's judgment; the protocol governs the split's recording, authorization, notification, review shape, and closure.

## 6. Evidence

**The deck gap.** The `issues` card's §4 sentence — *"Link sub-issues to parents; the hierarchy carries the authorization cascade and closure order"* — is the deck's only hierarchy text as of 2026-10-23, and no card states what the cascade or the closure order concretely is. The `plan` card's derivation guard (§4) covers traceability to a spec but has no rule for the spec ceasing to exist as such. The `work` card's authorization check (rule 2) tests whether authorization exists at the required scope and says nothing about a scope that is redistributed underneath a held authorization.

**The measured instances are ad hoc.** Every split and sibling pair in the store this deck governs — `.opencode#2210/#2211`, `.opencode#2622/#2623`, `.opencode#2658/#2659` — carries an empty link record (`links: []`, checked 2026-10-23): none registered its parent/sub relationship. `.opencode#2658`'s recomposition (store commit `c81089a5`, 2026-10-23, *"evidence moved to a research artifact, store-record repairs split out to #2659, normative spec reduced to ten requirements on three cards"*) redistributed content across the two surfaces with no registration and no protocol; the extraction kept the parent's own deliverable and left no open authorization, plan, or closure question — a spec-phase recomposition with nothing yet to void. The first post-approval recomposition will face those questions with no rule; this spec exists so the protocol is in place before that happens.

**The authorization ruling, stated this session.** The developer's split directive carried its authorization consequence: revoke prior implementation/PR authorization, and state explicitly that none existed in the session to revoke — the protocol's R-3 states the general rule (authorization does not survive the redistribution of its scope) and its spec-phase carve-out (nothing to void), with the notification (R-5) as the durable record of both.
