## Research findings backing the design (live sources, 2026-11-12)

- semver.org §9–§11: `X.Y.Z-dev.N` is legal pre-release syntax; numeric
  pre-release identifiers compare numerically (`dev.10 > dev.2`); a
  pre-release ranks below its release, so the release conversion is
  semver-native. https://semver.org/
- No OSS convention stamps literal versions on ordinary PRs; regular PRs
  carry bump-intent markers only (Conventional Commits titles, changeset
  files, semver labels). Literal versions appear on tool-generated release
  PRs. https://github.com/googleapis/release-please#whats-a-release-pr ,
  https://www.conventionalcommits.org/en/v1.0.0/
- In-tree dev-version precedent: Maven SNAPSHOT (in-tree, converted to clean
  version at release), PEP 440 `.devN`, Cargo `-dev`. release-please's Maven
  strategy runs snapshot-bump PRs labeled `autorelease: snapshot`.
  https://maven.apache.org/guides/getting-started/index.html#What_is_a_SNAPSHOT_VERSION ,
  https://peps.python.org/pep-0440/ ,
  https://github.com/googleapis/release-please/blob/main/docs/java.md
- First-version grounds: 0.1.0 initial-development convention (semver FAQ,
  npm, Cargo, changesets stub-then-minor path) vs 1.0.0 stability-commitment
  precedent for repos with production history (Go 1 announcement, go.dev
  release history: date-based weeklies → go1). PEP 440's epoch mechanism
  considered and rejected — sort-order compatibility with dead date tags is
  not required. https://go.dev/blog/go1 , https://go.dev/ref/mod
